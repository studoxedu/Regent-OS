The problem

Nobody actually knows when a child arrives.

Paper registers get forged. Tap cards create queues and get forgotten. Parents are left guessing, and schools are left fielding phone calls.

For parents

Constant guesswork

No confirmation their child reached school safely, and no alert if a child leaves early. Just worry.

For schools

Disputes & interruptions

Endless "has my child arrived?" calls, and no dispute-proof record when attendance is questioned.

For both

Systems that add friction

Tap-cards and biometrics make children stop, queue, and remember. At a busy gate, they simply fail.

How it works

One sticker. One reader. Zero effort from the child.

1

Each child wears a passive RFID sticker

A small, cheap tag goes on the backpack, uniform collar, or shoe. No battery, no charging — it lasts for years and costs about ₦300.

2

One long-range reader sits at the gate

A weatherproof reader mounted on a post detects tags from 2–5 metres away — and reads a whole class walking through together, all at once.

3

The child just walks through

No stopping. No tapping. No queue. The local hub logs the arrival or departure with a timestamp the instant they pass.

4

The parent gets a text in seconds

"Chidi arrived at God's Grace School at 7:52 AM." Delivered by SMS to any phone — no smartphone or app required on the parent's side.

5

The school sees everything

A live dashboard shows who's in and who's out, with exportable, timestamped logs that settle any attendance dispute instantly.

Why long-range

Built for a busy gate, not a quiet doorway.

Short-range tap systems were built for one person at a turnstile. Herald was built for 200 children arriving in half an hour.
